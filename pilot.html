FREQUENTLY ASKED

Before you ask.

How long does a KYVAR pilot take?

30–60 days on one real, non-critical workload. The pilot starts read-only, then opens one narrow, allowlisted write path with human approval — enough to measure fewer incidents, faster recovery and zero ungoverned changes.

What do we need to provide for a pilot?

One team, one connector, and an operational loop with a measurable baseline. The runtime needs a small host in your environment: 2 vCPU, 4 GB RAM, 20 GB disk, Docker with Compose, and outbound HTTPS.

Does KYVAR need inbound access to our network?

No. The runtime polls KYVAR over outbound HTTPS on port 443 — no inbound firewall rule, port forwarding, DMZ or VPN. Credentials can remain on the runtime inside your network.

Can KYVAR make changes without human approval?

Writes are deny-by-default. Risk policy decides when a human approval is required, the requester can never approve their own change, and high-risk real writes are refused outright. Every action leaves audit evidence from intent to outcome.

Which platforms does KYVAR support?

AWS, Microsoft Azure, Oracle Cloud (OCI), VMware, Linux, Windows, Kubernetes, Docker, Terraform, GitHub and Entra ID — ten connector families through one governed pipeline.
