HOW IT IS PRICED

A platform fee, plus the capacity to act.

Two lines and one pass-through. The platform fee buys the governance — approvals, RBAC, tenancy, audit, discovery, the whole control plane. Governed runtimes are the meter: each one is a unit of real, audited execution capacity inside your network, so what you pay tracks how much of your estate KYVAR actually operates. Model tokens are at cost — bring your own provider key and we never touch the bill, or we meter them at cost. A predictable fee beside variable model spend is what stops an AI-heavy month becoming a surprise for either side, and the per-day token budget is already in the product as the guardrail.

BEFORE YOU ASK

The questions procurement sends first.

Why not price per user?

Because the whole point is that one operator — or the system itself — does the work of many. A per-seat meter would tax exactly the outcome you are buying, and our revenue would fall as the product succeeded. Seats are a fair-use floor, never the meter.

Are AI model costs included?

No, deliberately. Either you bring your own provider key and we never touch the bill, or we meter tokens at cost. Bundling model spend into a platform fee means one of us is guessing at your usage and pricing the risk in. The daily token budget is built into the product, so a runaway month is bounded rather than discovered on an invoice.

Is there a free tier?

You can run KYVAR in mock mode at no cost and walk the entire flow — intent, risk classification, approval, execution, audit — without it touching real infrastructure. Governed execution against real infrastructure is the paid line, because that is where both the value and the liability sit.

What does the free pilot include?

Our time, at no charge: scoping the workload, standing up the runtime in your environment, connecting one system, agreeing the success criteria, and running the loop with you until the numbers are in. You pay nothing for the pilot, and whether or not you go on to a paid package, you keep an outcome report you can act on.

Can we run it entirely in our own environment?

Yes — that is the default, not an enterprise upsell. The control plane and the runtimes both deploy in your environment; a runtime opens no inbound ports and is the only place your credentials are ever decrypted. Air-gapped deployments are supported and are a conversation about your model provider, not about KYVAR.

What happens at renewal if we grew?

Governed runtimes are the meter, so growth is visible to both of us before renewal rather than discovered in an audit. We would rather agree an expansion when you add the fourth cloud than send a true-up letter about it a year later.
