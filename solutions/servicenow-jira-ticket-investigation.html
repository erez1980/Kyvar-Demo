For the service desk

AI investigation for every ticket.
Written back to ServiceNow and Jira.

KYVAR picks up tickets from Jira Service Management and ServiceNow, investigates them read-only, and writes the evidence back. A fix it proposes waits for a person.

The problem

Each ticket starts from zero.

Most of the time on an L1 or L2 ticket goes to gathering the same evidence: which VM, which service, what changed, has this happened before. Resolution notes are thin, the knowledge base falls behind, and the next engineer repeats the work.

The approach

Investigate, propose, approve, record.

Intake from the queue

New tickets from Jira Service Management or ServiceNow are investigated automatically, by the categories you choose.

Read-only investigation

The AI uses read-only tools under the requester’s own access and posts evidence-ranked hypotheses with confidence to the ticket.

Three remediation modes

Recommend only, carry out with approval, or pre-approved by an autonomy policy you define. ServiceNow Change Requests can be raised for the fix.

Knowledge that writes itself

Resolved incidents become knowledge-article drafts, reviewed by a person and published to Confluence or ServiceNow.

Architecture

Your service desk stays the system of record.

KYVAR connects to your service desk as a connector and writes its findings, approvals and outcome back to the ticket. The investigation runs on a runtime near the systems involved, over outbound HTTPS only. See the architecture page.

Security

Ticket text is data, not instructions.

Attacked on purpose

A ticket title can carry text written to steer an AI. Those paths are part of a 54-attack red-team suite that runs on every change.

Read before write

Investigations are read-only. A change is judged by what it would do, and a person sits on the approval gate.

Credentials stay away from the model

Credentials are encrypted, delivered per run to the runtime, and never returned by an API. Control-plane tokens are stripped from agent processes.

The full list is on the security page.

An example

A ticket that arrives with its answer.

An illustrative run:

  1. A user opens a Jira Service Management request: the intranet site is down.
  2. KYVAR investigates read-only: the VM state, the web service, recent changes and similar past incidents.
  3. It posts its finding and confidence to the ticket and proposes restarting the service.
  4. An engineer approves. The runtime runs the change, the site is checked again, and the ticket is updated and resolved.
  5. A knowledge-article draft from the incident waits for review.
Frequently asked

Questions buyers ask first.

Which service desks does KYVAR work with?

Jira Service Management and ServiceNow. KYVAR reads tickets, investigates them, and writes findings, approvals and outcomes back to the ticket.

Can KYVAR close tickets by itself?

Only in the mode you choose. Remediation can be recommend only, carried out with approval, or pre-approved by an autonomy policy, and closing a ticket is a setting per category.

Does the AI see our credentials?

No. Credentials are encrypted at rest, delivered per run to the runtime that owns the connector, and never returned by an API. Investigations run under the requester’s own access with read-only tools.

What if a ticket contains instructions aimed at the AI?

Ticket text is treated as data. KYVAR runs a 54-attack red-team suite on every change, including ticket titles, and any fix still waits for a person on the approval gate.

Does it help with our knowledge base?

Yes. Resolved incidents become knowledge-article drafts that a person reviews before they are published to Confluence or ServiceNow.
