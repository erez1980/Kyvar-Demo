AI operations for MSPs.
Every customer kept apart.

One governed console across your customers: each with its own tenant, runtime, ServiceNow and sign-in. AI investigates the queue, a person approves the fix, and both you and the customer can read the record.

The problem

More customers, the same engineers.

An MSP engineer works across many customers in one shift, each with its own service desk, credentials, change rules and escalation path. Scripts get copied from one customer to the next, and so do mistakes. When a customer asks what was done on their systems last night, the answer is spread across a ticket, a chat thread and somebody’s shell history.

Context switching

Every ticket starts with finding the right customer, console and credential before any real work begins.

Cross-customer risk

One automation with the wrong target, or one credential used in the wrong tenant, is a contract problem, not a bug.

Proof for the customer

Customers increasingly ask for evidence of who changed what, who approved it, and whether it worked.

The approach

One console. A boundary per customer.

A tenant per customer

A customer switcher in one console, with per-customer runtimes, connectors and policy. MSP roles decide who works on which customers.

The customer’s own ServiceNow

KYVAR polls each customer’s ServiceNow queue and handles tickets per customer and category: pull and investigate, recommend a fix, or approve and then execute. Change Requests and knowledge-article drafts are written back.

Investigation before action

The AI investigates with read-only tools under the requester’s own access and posts evidence-ranked findings. A fix it proposes still crosses the approval gate.

Audit on both sides

The MSP sees everything it did across customers; each customer sees the record for its own tenant, including any support access it approved.

Architecture

A runtime next to each customer’s systems.

The control plane keeps identity, policy, approvals and audit. A runtime installed inside each customer’s network executes the work and polls KYVAR over outbound HTTPS 443, so no inbound firewall rule or VPN into the customer is needed. Each connector is bound to one approved runtime, and credentials can stay on that runtime. The same product runs as one service for several MSPs or as a dedicated deployment. More detail is on the architecture page.

Security

Isolation the database enforces too.

Tenant boundaries

Role and tenant are resolved from the database on every request; another customer’s objects return 404, and PostgreSQL row-level security refuses their rows beneath the application.

Sign-in per customer

Each MSP and customer can bring its own OIDC sign-in with group mapping. Support access to a customer is something the customer approves.

Suspend in one step

An MSP can suspend a customer, and an emergency stop halts every runtime for a tenant until a person releases it.

The full list is on the security page.

An example

One night, one customer, one record.

An illustrative run, as an MSP would see it:

  1. A high-priority incident lands in one customer’s ServiceNow queue at night. KYVAR pulls it because that customer and category are set to investigate.
  2. The investigation runs read-only on that customer’s runtime, inside their network, and writes its findings and confidence back to the ticket.
  3. It proposes a fix. Policy says a person must approve, so the on-call engineer gets the request and approves it.
  4. The runtime carries out the change, the original check is run again, and the ticket is updated with what ran and the result.
  5. The customer can open the same record in their own tenant. A knowledge-article draft waits for review so the next one is faster.
Frequently asked

Questions buyers ask first.

Can one customer’s data reach another customer?

No. Tenant and role are resolved from the database per request, cross-tenant objects return 404, and PostgreSQL row-level security refuses another tenant’s rows beneath the application’s own checks. Each connector and credential belongs to one customer’s runtime.

Does each customer need their own ServiceNow instance?

KYVAR works with each customer’s own ServiceNow instance, and the handling mode is set per customer and per category: pull and investigate, recommend, or approve and then execute, with closing tickets and drafting knowledge articles as options.

Can our customers sign in with their own identity provider?

Yes. Each MSP and customer can configure its own OIDC sign-in with group mapping, and support access to a customer is approved by that customer.

Can a large customer get a dedicated deployment?

Yes. The same product runs as one service for several MSPs or as a dedicated deployment, with the same governance either way.
